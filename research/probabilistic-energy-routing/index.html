Probabilistic Energy Routing for Reliable Energy Internet Networks

A reliability-aware routing framework for local Energy Internet networks under uncertain demand, component failures, congestion, and transmission losses.

⬇ Download Slides Related Paper ↗

Abstract

This research investigates reliability-aware energy routing in Local Energy Internet networks, where distributed energy resources, storage units, flexible loads, Energy Routers, and information systems are interconnected through an intelligent energy-sharing architecture. In this structure, energy and information can flow in a coordinated and bidirectional manner, enabling more adaptive operation of future energy systems.

The core contribution is a Probabilistic Energy Routing (PER) framework that evaluates candidate paths under uncertain demand, stochastic line loading, congestion, transmission losses, capacity constraints, line failures, and Energy Router failures. Instead of selecting only the shortest or lowest-loss route, PER identifies the most reliable feasible route under modeled operating conditions.

The framework combines graph-based path analysis, Monte Carlo simulation, normal load modeling, exponential failure modeling, and reliability indices such as EENS, PCFSO, and RRW. It was developed in the M.Sc. thesis and further presented in the 2024 conference paper on probabilistic energy routing in Local Energy Internet networks.

Research Challenge

Dynamic Line Loading

Transmission-line loading changes over time due to uncertain demand, renewable generation, and network operating conditions. In this research, existing line load is modeled probabilistically using a normal distribution.

Line and Router Failures

Lines and Energy Routers may fail because of equipment outages, disconnections, or operating stress. Their reliability is modeled using exponential failure distributions with constant failure rates during useful lifetime operation.

Capacity and Loss Limits

A selected route must satisfy capacity constraints and acceptable transmission-loss limits. Congested or overloaded paths can increase the probability of failure and expected energy not supplied.

Methodology

STEP — 01
GRAPH CONSTRUCTION

Convert the Local Energy Internet into a graph

The network topology is modeled as a weighted graph in which Energy Routers are nodes and transmission lines are edges. This representation makes it possible to analyze routes, identify critical components, and evaluate system connectivity.

Graph Theory Topology Modeling Energy Routers
STEP — 02
PATH ENUMERATION

Identify possible routing paths using DFS

Depth-First Search is used to extract feasible paths between selected source and demand Energy Routers. These paths are not selected only by distance; they are later evaluated using probabilistic reliability criteria.

DFS Candidate Paths Source–Demand Routing
STEP — 03
STOCHASTIC LOAD MODELING

Model line loading under uncertainty

Existing power on transmission lines is sampled from a normal distribution. This allows the routing algorithm to consider realistic variations in line loading before adding new power transfer requests.

Normal Distribution Line Loading Uncertainty
STEP — 04
FAILURE MODELING

Evaluate line and Energy Router availability

Component failures are modeled using exponential reliability functions. A route is considered operational only when all essential lines and Energy Routers along the path remain available.

Exponential Distribution Failure Rate Series Reliability
STEP — 05
MONTE CARLO ANALYSIS

Simulate stochastic operating scenarios

Monte Carlo simulation is used to evaluate thousands of operating scenarios. The conference paper reports 10,000 samples and 100 repetitions to reduce variance, while the thesis uses 10,000 scenarios for case-study evaluation.

Mathematical Formulation

◎ SYSTEM MODEL

Energy Routers as network nodes

In the proposed Local Energy Internet model, each Energy Router (ER) is represented as a network node, while transmission links between routers are represented as graph edges. This graph-based representation enables systematic path discovery, reliability analysis, and routing evaluation.

$$G=(V,E,W)$$ $$w_{ij} = \begin{cases} l_{ij} & i \neq j \\ l_i & i = j \\ \infty & [v_i, v_j] \notin E \end{cases}$$

where \(V\) is the set of nodes or Energy Routers, \(E\) is the set of transmission links, and \(W\) represents edge weights such as loss, resistance, cost, or routing-related parameters.

∑ PER MODEL

Reliability-aware route evaluation

The PER framework selects the most reliable feasible route by maximizing the probability that transmission losses remain within the allowable limit, the transferred power stays within path capacity, and all required components remain operational.

$$\operatorname*{Argmax}_{l \in M} \Pr\!\left(P_{loss,l}\leq P_0,\; P^{tr}_{ij}\leq Cap_{l},\; \psi_{state,l}=1\right)$$

where \(M\) is the set of candidate paths, \(P_{loss,l}\) is the power loss along path \(l\), \(P_0\) is the maximum allowable power loss, \(P^{tr}_{ij}\) is the transmitted power on line \(i-j\), \(Cap_l\) is the effective path capacity, and \(\psi_{state,l}\) represents the operational state of the selected path.

The total power loss of a candidate path is evaluated by combining Energy Router conversion loss and transmission-line loss along the selected route:

$$P_{loss,l}=\sum_{k_l=1}^{N}\left[(1-\eta_i)P_d+\frac{r_{ij}}{U_{ij}^{2}} \left((P^{tr}_{ij})^{2}-P_{ex,ij}^{2}\right)\right]_{k_l}$$

The transferred power on a line is calculated by adding the new demand request to the existing line power:

$$P^{tr}_{ij}=P^d+P^{ex}_{ij}$$

where \(P^d\) is the demanded power and \(P^{ex}_{ij}\) is the existing power on line \(i-j\). The existing line power is modeled using a normal distribution:

$$P^{ex}_{ij}\sim N(\mu_{ij},\sigma_{ij})$$

The effective capacity of a route segment is constrained by the line capacity and the capacities of the connected Energy Routers:

$$Cap_l=\min(Cap_{ij},Cap_i,Cap_j)$$

Therefore, a feasible transfer must satisfy:

$$P^{tr}_{ij}\leq Cap_l$$

Component reliability is modeled using the exponential reliability function:

$$R_i(t)=e^{-\lambda_i t}$$

For a series path, the route remains operational only if all required components remain operational:

$$R_s(t)=\prod_{i=1}^{N}R_i(t)$$

Reliability Metrics

METRIC 01

PCFSO

Probability of Component Failure and System Outage measures the probability that a component failure leads to load curtailment or system outage.

$$PCFSO_i^h=Pr(P_h>0\ \text{and}\ \psi_{state,l}=0)$$
METRIC 02

EENS / EENSCF

Expected Energy Not Supplied quantifies the expected amount of energy that cannot be delivered. In the paper, EENSCF is used to evaluate expected unsupplied energy after component failure.

$$EENSCF_i^h=EENS_h \times PCFSO_i^h$$
METRIC 03

RRW

Risk Reduction Worth ranks components based on their potential contribution to risk reduction and reliability improvement. .

Research Impact

↗ CONTRIBUTION

A reliability-aware basis for routing decisions in Local Energy Internet networks

The proposed framework provides a systematic way to evaluate and compare energy-routing paths under uncertainty. By integrating graph-based path discovery, stochastic load modeling, component failure analysis, and reliability metrics, the approach supports more informed decisions for congestion management, route selection, component reinforcement, and resource allocation.

The results indicate that PER can reduce the risk of selecting unreliable routes and can help identify components whose improvement may significantly enhance overall system reliability.
